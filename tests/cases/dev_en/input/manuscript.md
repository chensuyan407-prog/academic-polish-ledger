# Abstract

With the rapid development of renewable energy, perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted more and more attention. However, the long-term stability of PSC are still limited by moisture and heat. In this work, we report a fluorinated hole transporting layer that may suppress the ion migration at the interface. The champion device reach a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.4% and retains about 90% of its initial photoelectric conversion efficiency after 1000 h at 85 °C (Fig. 3b). These results suggests that interfacial fluorination is a promising strategy for stable devices [4, 7].

# Introduction

Perovskite photovoltaic cell have shown a huge progress in the past decade [1-3]. Their efficiencies increased from 3.8% to above 26% [2]. But the degradation under operating conditions don't allow commercial application. Several study have attributed the degradation to the migration of iodide ions, which is accelerated when temperature exceeds 60 °C [5]. A HTL with low ion permeability could therefore extend the device lifetime, if its energy level is matched with the perovskite.
