Background: Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is associated with increased cardiovascular risk, but the role of serum uric acid remains unclear.

Methods: We enrolled 1,248 patients with stage 3–4 CKD from 2015 to 2019. Patients were followed for a median of 4.2 years (IQR 3.1–5.0). Cox regression was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) after adjustment for age, sex and eGFR.

Results: Higher uric acid was associated with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) (HR 1.32, 95% CI 1.11–1.57; p = 0.002). The association was not significant in patients younger than 50 years. Allopurinol use did not modify the risk.

Conclusions: Elevated uric acid may be an independent risk factor for cardiovascular events in CKD, and randomised controlled trials are needed.
