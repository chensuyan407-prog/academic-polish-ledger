# Abstract

Perovskite solar cells (PSCs) have attracted increasing attention as a renewable energy technology. However, the long-term stability of PSCs is still limited by moisture and heat. In this work, we report a fluorinated hole transport layer that may suppress ion migration at the interface. The champion device reaches a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.4% and retains about 90% of its initial PCE after 1000 h at 85 °C (Fig. 3b). These results suggest that interfacial fluorination is a promising strategy for stable devices [4, 7].

# Introduction

Perovskite solar cells have shown considerable progress in the past decade [1-3]. Their efficiencies increased from 3.8% to above 26% [2]. However, degradation under operating conditions does not yet allow commercial application. Several studies have attributed the degradation to the migration of iodide ions, which is accelerated when the temperature exceeds 60 °C [5]. An HTL with low ion permeability could therefore extend the device lifetime if its energy level is matched to that of the perovskite.
