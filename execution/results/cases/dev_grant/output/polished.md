This project focuses on hard carbon anodes for sodium-ion batteries (SIBs) and aims to elucidate their sodium storage mechanism. Previous studies have indicated that the initial Coulombic efficiency (ICE) of hard carbon is only about 75%, which severely limits its practical application [3]. In our previous work, we found that moderate sulfur doping can increase the reversible capacity to approximately 320 mAh g-1 (Fig. 1). However, the effect of doping on the ICE remains unclear. This project will focus on the relationship between defect structure and sodium storage behavior, which is expected to provide a theoretical basis for the design of high-performance anode materials.
